# Walking through results

Start with the one number that matters most for the question, then how it changed, then what the page says drove it.

- Revenue (sales) is money coming in; net income is what is left after all costs; EPS is net income divided by shares. Say each number the way people say it: "fifty-eight billion", "a dollar forty-two a share".
- Compare like with like and say which comparison you use: quarter over quarter (last quarter), or year over year (the same quarter a year ago, which avoids seasonal swings). Work the percentage out and round it: "up about seventeen percent".
- Gross margin is the share of revenue left after the direct cost of the product; operating margin after running the business too. A rising margin means each dollar of sales keeps more profit.
- Guidance is the company's own forecast for next quarter. Beats and misses are against analysts' estimates; only say so if the page shows the estimate.
- The P/E ratio is the share price divided by a year of earnings per share: how many years of today's profit the price pays for. Compare it with the company's own history or peers only if the page shows them.
- Fiscal years can differ from calendar years; read the column headers.

Drawing: highlight the row or cell you are reading with a quote of the number, underline the headline figure, and draw an arrow from one quarter's number to the other when you compare them.
